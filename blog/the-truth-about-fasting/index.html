The Truth About Fasting

What Nobel Prize-winning science can teach us about mindset and transformation

  • By Marcus Thomlinson
  • 5 min read

In 2016, a quietly brilliant scientist named Yoshinori Ohsumi was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his groundbreaking work on a process that, until recently, flew under the radar of even the most science-savvy among us: autophagy.

At first glance, this discovery might seem deeply embedded in the world of biology, far removed from the everyday lives of people striving for clarity, purpose, and peace of mind.

But take a closer look, and what you’ll see is not just a scientific revelation - you’ll find a powerful metaphor for personal growth, inner renewal, and the resilience of the human spirit.

What is Autophagy?

Autophagy - from the Greek “auto” (self) and “phagein” (to eat) - literally means “self-eating.” It’s a natural process inside our cells where they identify old, damaged, or unnecessary components and break them down, recycle them, and use the raw materials to build something new.

It’s like spring cleaning on a cellular level - removing clutter, getting rid of the broken bits, and making room for vitality, adaptability, and improved health.

It’s not about destruction. It’s about renewal.

And the deeper you understand this process, the more it becomes a beautiful allegory for mindset and personal transformation.

Letting Go of What No Longer Serves You

In life, just like in our cells, we accumulate clutter - but not just physical clutter. We carry emotional baggage, limiting beliefs, toxic habits, and worn-out identities that no longer serve who we are becoming.

Autophagy teaches us that renewal doesn’t begin with adding more. It starts with letting go.

Letting go of outdated narratives. Letting go of perfectionism. Letting go of roles we’ve outgrown.

This isn’t weakness. It’s nature. It’s wisdom. And it’s how we evolve.

Ask yourself:

  • What belief am I holding onto that’s outlived its purpose?

  • What habit or thought pattern is draining my energy?

  • What part of me is ready to be transformed into something better?

Just like your body clears out damaged cells to make space for health, you can clear out mental and emotional clutter to make space for clarity, creativity, and confidence.

The Courage to Recycle the Old Into the New

Autophagy isn’t just about disposal. It’s about recycling - turning something old into something valuable. And this is where mindset truly matters.

When you face setbacks, failures, or even moments of crisis, the most empowering question you can ask is: “What can I take from this?”

Because within every disappointment is raw material for insight. Within every painful lesson is wisdom. Within every breakdown is the potential for a breakthrough.

Ohsumi’s research showed us that our cells are more resourceful than we ever imagined. You are too.

Fasting, Focus, and Finding Your Centre

Interestingly, one of the ways scientists discovered autophagy is through the study of fasting. When cells are starved of nutrients, they kick into a survival mode where they clean up and recycle what they already have.

In our lives, this principle can show up in quiet moments of pause - when we stop filling our days with noise, consumption, and endless doing.

Sometimes the greatest clarity and growth comes not from adding more, but from intentionally stepping back. Saying no. Creating space.

Whether it’s a digital detox, a few minutes of silence in the morning, or a day spent journaling, these moments give us access to the “inner autophagy” of the soul - a process of letting go and reorienting around what truly matters.

Self-Care as Cellular Intelligence

Autophagy is a self-care mechanism at the most fundamental level of your being.

Your body was designed with the intelligence to heal, restore, and regenerate - even when things have gone off course. And your mind and spirit are no different.

When you feel stuck, overwhelmed, or like you’re carrying the weight of too many unfinished versions of yourself - know this: renewal is built into your nature.

But just like in biology, you must activate it.

That might mean:

  • Creating space for rest and reflection

  • Saying goodbye to toxic relationships

  • Embracing new learning

  • Letting go of the need to be “always on”

This is not indulgent. This is intelligent.

What Autophagy Means for Your Mindset

Yoshinori Ohsumi didn’t just discover a cellular process. He helped us realise that cleansing, renewal, and growth are not luxuries - they are necessities.

Mindset is not just about positive thinking. It’s about inner alignment - continually updating your inner world to stay in tune with who you are becoming.

That requires inner autophagy:

  • Identifying limiting beliefs

  • Disassembling old thought loops

  • Recycling past failures into future wisdom

  • Rebuilding from the inside out

And just like autophagy helps cells survive and thrive under pressure, cultivating a mindset of renewal helps you navigate stress, challenge, and change with grace and resilience.

Your Invitation to Inner Renewal

The next time you feel overwhelmed, stagnant, or on the edge of a life transition, remember this:

You are not broken. You are not lost. You are in a process of renewal.

You’re not here to be perfect. You’re here to evolve - and like every living cell in your body, you were made to adapt, release, rebuild and thrive.

Let Yoshinori Ohsumi’s work remind you: transformation isn’t a bonus - it’s biology.

And if your cells can do it, so can you.

Final Thought

We often think of science and mindset as separate worlds - but they’re not. The wisdom in your body reflects the wisdom in your life. Ohsumi’s Nobel-winning discovery of autophagy isn’t just a triumph for medicine. It’s a quiet, powerful reminder of nature’s greatest truth:

The ability to renew is already within you.

So today, choose one thing to release - and one step toward your own evolution.

Your body already knows how. Your mindset can too.

A quick note: this post is for general information and inspiration. It is not medical advice. Please speak to your GP before trying fasting, especially if you have a medical condition, take medication, are pregnant, or have ever had a difficult relationship with food.
